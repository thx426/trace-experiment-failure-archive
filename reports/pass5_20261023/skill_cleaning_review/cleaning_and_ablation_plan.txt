STATUS: review proposal; no live config, frozen experiment, or queued job changed.

Question
Does incorrect guidance or task-specific privileged information reduce learning or unaided generalization? Existing smoke results do not identify either causal effect.

Information boundary
- Allowed: user-requested attributes, the executor's public action schema, observations already received, and accepted action history.
- Remove from the procedural Skill: preselected product IDs, hidden target mappings, success labels, final answer paths, later observations, and unsupported conclusions from researcher trials.
- A product ID appearing in current search results is public at that point. Knowing that it is the correct answer before inspecting it is a separate informational advantage. Literal ID exposure alone cannot certify the absence of semantic privilege.
- Delete false claims and unsupported universal bans. Do not convert a malformed action's failure into a claim that an environment feature does not work.
- Retain conditional procedures: observable trigger -> available action -> verification condition -> fallback. Do not erase actionable strategy when removing identifiers.
- The earlier skill_*_review_draft.txt files contain task-specific answers and are diagnostic repair drafts. They are not the answer-free training condition.

First comparison: hold the student checkpoint and sampling mechanism fixed
A: no Skill.
B: original raw Skill.
C: fact-checked concise Skill retaining task-specific candidate hints.
D: fact-checked concise procedural Skill without privileged task-specific hints.
C vs D is the key privilege comparison; B vs C primarily probes quality. Match non-privileged instructions, document/token budget where possible, intervention settings, task pool, and evaluation budget. Report residual length/content confounding.
Select training tasks across low/middle/high strata; do not select only the two reviewed failures. Keep development/test labels out of Skill generation and selection. Evaluate on fresh attempts. Count researcher experiments and any extra rollout sampling separately.
Measure immediate success, mixed-reward group fraction, invalid/repeated actions, missing-option purchases, sampling intervention amount, and cost. Then run matched training only for promising variants, with unaided held-out evaluation as the primary endpoint. Do not infer learning from guided rollout success alone.

Second comparison: reward signal, independently of Skill content
The current experiment uses binary success reward and no within-group std normalization. All-failure groups have zero task-reward advantage even if their environment scores differ.
Compare binary reward with an explicitly declared partial-score reward condition across both no-Skill and procedural-Skill arms. A possible pilot objective is success_indicator + alpha * bounded_environment_score, with alpha predeclared and separately validated. This is a changed training objective, not a silent fix. Keep evaluation success unchanged. Track partial-score purchases without full success to detect proxy optimization.
Evaluate bounded dynamic sampling of nonconstant-reward groups as a separate ablation. Do not silently drop difficult tasks from evaluation or hide resampling costs. Do not repeatedly sample until success without a budget cap.

Mechanism diagnostics
- Task-reward advantage distribution and mixed-reward group fraction.
- Group success diversity, distinguished from merely different wording/actions.
- Guided versus bare rollout success and unaided validation gain.
- Behavior-policy accounting, importance-weight concentration/effective sample size, and PPO clip fraction, when applicable to the selected SPS configuration.
- Successful and failed trajectory lengths. norm_adv_by_std=false removes only the std-normalization component; it does not imply the full Dr.GRPO objective or resolve all length weighting effects.
